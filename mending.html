Mending

How to fix the things you own, and who to take them to when you can't. Organised by what's gone wrong, not by trade. London first.

Do it yourself Either Take it to someone

Stains

Something spilt tonight

Cold water, blot, don't rub, and no heat until it's gone. Most stains are lost in the first hour, not the wash.

 A stain that has been there for months

Set-in oil and tannin can still shift with the right solvent and patience. If it's silk, wool suiting or anything you'd mind ruining, a specialist cleaner is cheaper than a mistake.

 Grease on wool or tweed

Fuller's earth or cornflour overnight, brush out, repeat. Wool forgives more than people expect.

 Mould on a wax jacket

White vinegar, a soft brush and an afternoon outdoors, then re-wax. It comes from being stored damp, so fix the cupboard as well.

 Marks on suede or nubuck

Suede punishes home remedies. A cleaner who does suede regularly, and accept it may come back a shade darker.

Knitwear

Moth holes in a jumper

A darn is an evening's work and the most forgiving repair there is. If it's cashmere and you want it to vanish, an invisible mender will rebuild the knit from about £90 a hole.

 Thinning elbows and cuffs

Reinforce before it goes. Swiss darning over the worn patch, or a contrast darn if you'd rather it showed.

Waxed cotton

Dry, faded wax jacket

A tin of wax, a hairdryer and a Sunday afternoon. The most common problem in the category and the easiest to solve.

 Torn or worn-through waxed cotton

Patching wax properly means matching cloth and heat-sealing. A specialist waxed-cotton repairer, or the maker's own service.

 Sticky or rotten tartan lining

Replacing a lining is a full strip-down. Budget for it or decide the jacket has earned its retirement.

Denim and workwear

Blown-out crotch or thigh

Machine darning over an internal patch. Doable at home with a sewing machine and patience; a denim repair bench will do it for £25–£60 with a few weeks' wait.

 Hems and chainstitching

A plain hem anyone can do. A chainstitch hem needs a Union Special, and there are only a handful in London.

Fastenings

Broken zip

A lost slider is a five-minute fix with a replacement pull. A zip that's torn from the tape needs the whole thing replaced, which is a tailor's job.

 Missing or loose buttons

Learn to do this properly once, with a thread shank, and you'll never pay for it again.

Shoes and leather

Worn soles and heels

Welted shoes are built to be resoled; do it before you wear through the welt. Glued soles are usually not worth it.

 Dry, scuffed leather

Clean, condition, polish. Most leather that looks finished just needs feeding.

Hats, gloves and the rest

Hat lost its shape

A re-block over a wooden form is a hatter's job, and London still has hatters who do it.

 Split glove seam

Fine leather seams want a glover's needle and someone who's done a thousand of them.

Each entry links to the people who do the work, with what they charge and what they won't take on. Entries carry the date they were last checked, because workshops move, pause and close.
