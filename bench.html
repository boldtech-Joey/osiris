The Bench

Garments that came to us broken and left fixed. Every piece is mended properly, marked, and given a number so you can see exactly what was done to it and by whom. Nothing is hidden and nothing is pretended new.

Every bench piece is stitched with a closed eye in oxidised orange, somewhere discreet, and carries a woven label with its number. Scan the label and you get the piece's record: what came in, what was done, and what to do when it needs mending again. When it comes back to be mended again, and it will, we add to it.

Barbour Bedale, sage

Chest 42 (C42). Fits a 40 to 42. Made in South Shields, mid-1990s.

Came in from a house clearance in Suffolk with a ripped left pocket, a lining gone tacky at the collar and a decade's worth of not being waxed. Mechanically sound underneath. Three weeks on the bench.

What was fixed, by Tom Hale 4
  • Left hand-warmer pocket, torn through
    Tear closed from the inside with a heat-sealed waxed cotton patch, colour-matched to the faded sage rather than the original.
  • Whole jacket re-waxed
    Old wax lifted with a hairdryer and cloth, then two coats of thornproof dressing worked in warm and cured overnight.
  • Collar lining replaced
    Tartan cotton lining cut from a donor Bedale of the same era and re-set by hand along the collar seam.
  • Zip slider
    Original brass slider was cracked. Replaced with a period-correct brass slider on the original zip tape.

Bench 0012 Marked with the closed eye inside the left cuff, in oxidised orange thread.

French moleskin work jacket, ink blue

Marked 52, fits a 40. Le Laboureur, 1970s or 80s.

A bleu de travail with the usual story: elbows worn to lace, two buttons gone, moth had a go at the collar. The cloth still has years in it.

What was fixed, by Priya Nair 3
  • Both elbows worn through
    Visible darn in a slightly lighter blue wool, so the repair reads as a repair. About four hours each.
  • Three moth holes at the collar
    Swiss-darned in matching cotton so they disappear.
  • Two buttons missing
    Replaced with vintage French corozo buttons from the drawer, all six re-sewn on a thread shank.

Bench 0013 Marked with the closed eye inside the front facing, in oxidised orange thread.

Shetland jumper, oatmeal

Chest 42, unlabelled, hand-framed. Probably 1980s.

Bought at Kilburn for four pounds with seven moth holes. Good tight wool, the kind that gets better with a wash and a beating on the line.

What was fixed, by Valentina Karellas 2
  • Seven moth holes, front and one sleeve
    Darned in the same oatmeal wool. Five invisible, two left as small deliberate contrast darns because they were on the cuff and we liked them.
  • Neck rib stretched
    Re-blocked damp over a form and steamed back to size.

Bench 0014 Marked with the closed eye at the inside hem, in oxidised orange thread.
